Four worlds
· one wilderness
Four worlds · 01

The sal forest

Deciduous forest · Shorea robusta

The backbone of Bardiya. Tall, straight sal trees form a canopy covering much of the park, and in the shade beneath it the largest predators move. A cathedral of trunks and filtering light.

Four worlds · 02

The savanna

Phantas · elephant grass

The open plains Bardiya calls phantas, an ocean of grass taller than a person. Rhinos and deer graze here, and this is where the tiger hunts in the golden light, visible hundreds of meters away.

Four worlds · 03

The jungle

Riverine forest · Babai & Karnali

Where the Babai and Karnali rivers run, the forest grows thickest. Fast-growing trees and impenetrable undergrowth form green corridors along the water. Life is richest here.

Four worlds · 04

Floden

Karnali & Babai · flodsystem

The river's branches spin a web of side channels, sandbanks, and marshes. The artery of life, where gharial and mugger crocodile lie on the banks and the Ganges river dolphin breaks the surface. Without the water, none of the rest exists.

Djurriket
· the species sharing the wilderness
Vulnerable
Panthera pardus

Leopard

The other cat, and a master of staying unseen. In Bardiya the leopard lives in the tiger's shadow, hunting the fringes she doesn't frequent, mostly at night. It drags its kill up into the trees, away from both tiger and hyena.

Vulnerable
Melursus ursinus

Sloth bear

Shaggy, black, and unpredictable. It tears open termite mounds with claws as long as fingers and sucks up the insects through a gap in its teeth. Considered more dangerous to meet on foot than the tiger, since it's nearsighted and easily startled.

Vulnerable
Rhinoceros unicornis

Greater one-horned rhinoceros

Two tons in what looks like riveted armor. The grey skin folds like armor plates, a prehistoric animal that grazes the wetlands at dawn and dusk. Nearly wiped out in Nepal fifty years ago, now back in Bardiya's grass.

Endangered
Elephas maximus

Asian elephant

The giant that moves through the park in family groups led by the oldest female, along paths their mothers walked before them. Bardiya was once home to Rajagaj, the tallest Asian elephant ever measured. Some males roam alone and can be unpredictable during musth.

Chapter 03 · The inhabitants · 2 of 4

Floden

The Karnali carries glacial water through the park, and a whole world of its own lives within it.
Vulnerable

Mugger crocodile

Crocodylus palustris

The gharial's broad-jawed neighbor, a generalist that takes everything from fish to mammals at the water's edge. It lies motionless on the sandbanks with its jaws open to cool down, and digs long burrows in the riverbank where it overwinters.

Near threatened

Golden mahseer

Tor putitora

The Himalayas' silver dragon, a powerful carp that can grow over a meter long and fights so hard it's called the subcontinent's premier game fish. It migrates upriver to spawn, and its presence signals that the water is still clean and healthy.

Critically endangered
Gavialis gangeticus

Gharial

The river dragon with a snout as narrow as a sword, built to snap through the water and catch fish. The male grows a round knob on the tip of his snout that amplifies his hissing call. Critically endangered globally, with only a few hundred adults left in the wild, and the Karnali river is one of its very last strongholds.

Endangered
Platanista gangetica

Ganges river dolphin

The freshwater ghost, almost completely blind since its eyes lack a lens, it sees the world through echo. You hear it before you see it: a "pffft" as it breaks the surface to breathe. Nepal's national aquatic animal, and one of the rarest dolphins on Earth.

Chapter 03 · The inhabitants · 3 of 4

The rare & elusive

The animals you might never see.
Endangered

Dhole

Cuon alpinus

The Asian wild dog, neither wolf nor fox but its own genus. It doesn't bark, it whistles, and a pack hunts with such coordination it can bring down prey ten times its own size. Seeing one is rare enough to justify the whole trip.

Vulnerable

King cobra

Ophiophagus hannah

The world's longest venomous snake, up to five meters, and the only snake that builds a nest for its eggs. It feeds almost exclusively on other snakes. When it feels threatened, it raises a third of its body straight up to meet you at eye level, an encounter few forget.

Near threatened

Striped hyena

Hyaena hyaena

The lone wolf of hyenas, smaller and shyer than its African relatives. It moves alone at dusk with jaws strong enough to crush bones no other predator can manage. Rarely seen, more often just heard as a sound in the dark at the edge of the park.

Critically endangered

Chinese pangolin

Manis pentadactyla

A mammal covered in scales made of the same material as your fingernails, which rolls into a ball when threatened. It lives on ants and termites and moves mostly at night. Sadly the world's most trafficked mammal, which makes every sighting in Bardiya something precious.

Chapter 03 · The inhabitants · 4 of 4

The birds

Over 400 species fill the air. Here are the ones that stay with you.
Least concern

Indian peafowl

Pavo cristatus

The jungle's alarm clock. The male's cry echoes through the sal forest at dawn, and when he fans his tail into a blue and green eye of feathers, few sights compare. Peafowl also warn of predators, so a sudden shriek can mean the tiger is on the move.

Vulnerable

Great hornbill

Buceros bicornis

The beak giant, with a yellow helmet-like growth on top of its bill. Its wingbeats roar so loudly you hear the bird before you see it. The female seals herself into a hollow tree while incubating, and the male feeds her through a narrow slit until the chicks are ready.

Critically endangered

Bengal florican

Houbaropsis bengalensis

One of the rarest birds in the world, with only a few hundred left. In spring the male performs a display flight, throwing himself up out of the tall grass in a fluttering dance to be seen by the females. Dependent on exactly the kind of untouched grassland Bardiya protects.

Vulnerable

Sarus crane

Antigone antigone

The world's tallest flying bird, nearly the height of a person with a red head glowing against the grass. The sarus crane mates for life, and pairs are almost always seen together in the wetlands. In the region it's a symbol of fidelity and happiness.

The Ruler
· the lone one
The inhabitants · the ruler
Panthera tigris tigris · starkt hotad

Bengal tiger

The ruler, and the reason you're here. Bardiya has one of the highest tiger densities on the entire subcontinent, denser per square kilometer than almost anywhere else. Here you don't track her from a jeep but on foot, through grass taller than a person. A male can weigh 250 kilograms and move silently three steps from you without you noticing a thing.

The Village
· the people who live in it
Full image · the village
The people of the Terai

People of the forest

The villages sit right where the wilderness begins, and life in them has a rhythm that's disappeared almost everywhere else. Houses of mud and bamboo, painted with flowers and animals. Rice and lentils from the fields, fish from the river, evenings around the fire. And in the grass a few hundred meters away, the tigers move, denser here than almost anywhere else. Spending time in the village is a big part of the experience.

This is what makes Bardiya a genuinely one-of-a-kind place. You don't look into this world through a jeep window, you step into it: staying with a family, eating at their table, waking to the village morning with the jungle as your neighbor. In the guide I'll show you where to stay and how to get here.

Tiger season Nature season Rainy season

Between March and May you have the best chance of seeing tigers. It's the dry season, the grass has burned off and is shorter, the heat drives the animals to the last waterholes, and visibility is at its best. Avoid the monsoon from June to September when everything floods.

Temperature and weather

Tiger seasonDay 30–38°C · Night 15–20°C

Most outings happen early in the morning or in the afternoon when it's cool. When you're on a motorbike or in a jeep, the wind makes it even cooler. I recommend a room with AC and resting in the middle of the day for a comfortable stay. Further down you'll find recommendations on what to wear.

Visa & vaccines

01

Visa for Nepal

As a Swedish citizen you get a "Visa-on-Arrival". Something you can get through a short form via the kiosks you're met with right when you land in Kathmandu, or via your phone. Link: nepaliport.immigration.gov.np ↗ (Nepal's official immigration portal).

  • Choose: (insert correct category)
  • 15 days: around 30 USD
  • 30 days: around 50 USD
  • Bring: passport valid for at least 6 months
  • Bring: cash in USD for the fee
I got my Visa-on-Arrival via my phone after landing. The staff were friendly and helpful, and there were signs everywhere with instructions, so it felt easy. You could also withdraw cash at the counter, but I recommend having it ready so you save time and don't have to run around asking.

02

Vaccines & health

No vaccinations are required for entry. But if you want protection against various illnesses, book an appointment at a vaccination clinic well in advance, some require several doses spaced weeks apart.

Book your vaccination consultation 6–8 weeks before the trip. That way all doses have time to be completed.

The activities
Experience 01
On foot · the flagship

Walking Safari

The heart of Bardiya. Tracking tiger on foot, with just a guide and the silence, is an entirely different thing than sitting in a jeep. You read tracks in the mud, listen for the alarm calls, and feel the jungle breathing around you. Nothing else comes close to this feeling.

~370 SEK/day
Experience 02
On wheels · further in

Jeep Safari

Covers more ground and reaches deeper into the park than your feet can manage. Best for scanning the vast grasslands and reaching the river viewpoints, where the tiger often appears in the heat as it comes down to drink.

~800 SEK/day
Experience 03
Under the open sky · the night

Jeep safari & camping

A package of two days of jeep safari and one night of camping in the jungle, together with the Homestay crew. The crew needs to be there to make sure the tiger doesn't dare come close. Take the chance to play cards, hang out, and hear their experiences and stories. Take a dip in the Karnali river and enjoy the nature. The sounds after dark are a world of their own, and the dawn as the park wakes up gives you early access to the animal kingdom. This doesn't increase your chances of seeing wildlife, but it does give you the chance to say you've camped in a tiger jungle. I actually saw a tiger near the camp, so it turned into an incredibly powerful experience!

~5,600 SEK

Tip: Share the experience with others in the group, it brings the price per person down significantly.

Experience 04
Among the branches · the dawn

Night in a treehouse

Up among the branches, surrounded by the savanna. In the tall grass a tigress and her two cubs are hiding. Get embedded in nature at a safe distance with a panoramic view, looking out over the almost alien landscape where drought and green overlap. If you're lucky, the animal kingdom steps out of the tall grass. This is where I witnessed a successful tiger hunt.

The Travel Guide · Before you go

Mindset

Four things to keep in mind before you land, so the trip meets you where you are.
01

Calibrate your expectations

The tiger is extremely rare, only a few hundred remain in Nepal. Bardiya's open grasslands mean the chance of actually seeing one is among the best in Nepal, but it always happens on nature's terms. If you travel with the expectation of experiencing the village and the nature itself, you'll always come home with magical memories. Some go and see no tiger at all, others see several in a single day.

02

Be flexible, don't book everything in advance

It's not recommended to book all activities in advance. Stay open, listen to the guides' advice. They want you to go home with the best experiences, and trust their judgment. Keep the days flexible for adjustments based on their recommendations. Word spreads fast in the village when the tiger's been spotted, and you may need to change plans to get your chance.

03

Not like Tanzania or Kenya

The wildlife is magical in Bardiya but differs from classic safaris in Tanzania and Kenya, where you can see hundreds of animals at once. The forest is dense and the grass is tall, and the animals like to hide. That's what makes every sighting more valuable here.

04

Bardiya is wild, for real

The line between wilderness and village life is almost nonexistent. The risk is real, and you need to be aware of it. The guides are knowledgeable and look out for your safety, but this is no fenced-in zoo. You travel at your own risk.
